# healthy-minds-loneliness
QSS 20 final project on loneliness and mental health among emerging adults

# Overview
My project examines how loneliness shapes mental health outcomes among U.S. college students. I used the Healthy Minds Study 2024-2025 to see how strongly depression (PHQ- 9) and anxiety (GAD-7) co-occur with loneliness. How does loneliness relate to depressive symptoms and other mental health outcomes among college students, and how do these relationships vary across the full range of loneliness in this population? I am also interested in seeing if these patterns differ by identity–such as sexual orientation, race, or social class–to see whether or not these identities buffer or make individuals more vulnerable to loneliness. Lastly, I am interested in possible protective or risk factors strongly correlated with loneliness, such as societal and self stigma.

# Research Questions
1. How does loneliness relate to depressive and anxiety symptoms among emerging adults?
2. Do these relationships differ by sexual orientation, race, or other identity factors? Do campus belonging and stigma act as protective or risk factors?

# Data
The Healthy Minds Network. A premier research organization, they use a population-level survey of college student mental health. Over 935,000 responses from students at more than 675 colleges and universities have been gathered. I requested the data online and received the datasets for the year 2024-2025 (84,735 respondents).

# Key findings
- 31.6% of emerging adults screen positive for depression (PHQ-9 ≥ 10)
- 28.8% screen positive for anxiety (GAD-7 ≥ 10)
- 44.2% are lonely (UCLA-3 ≥ 6)
- Depression and anxiety correlate strongly (r = 0.74), and both correlate 
  moderately with loneliness (r = 0.54 and r = 0.47)
- Mental health outcomes scale strongly with loneliness levels
